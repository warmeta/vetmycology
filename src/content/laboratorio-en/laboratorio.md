DIAGNOSIS OF CRYPTOCOCCOSIS

Cryptococcosis is a disease caused by *Cryptococcus neoformans*, which has a worldwide distribution. The disease follows a subacute to chronic course,

affecting both humans and

various animal species (horse, sheep, goat, cat, cow, dog, pig, etc.). The organs most commonly affected in dogs are the central nervous system, eyes, respiratory tract, skin and subcutaneous tissue.

In cats it usually presents as a form of multiple lymphadenitis, although this also occurs in dogs. Clinically it may manifest with one or more of the following symptoms or signs:

neurological symptoms,

ocular signs, anorexia, progressive weight loss, cutaneous lesions, fever, generalised lymphadenopathy, dry cough, nasal discharge, vomiting and osteolysis.

The type of sample that should be sent to the

laboratory must be

whole blood contained in tubes without

any type of anticoagulant. In the presence of neurological symptoms, the sample must be cerebrospinal fluid.
